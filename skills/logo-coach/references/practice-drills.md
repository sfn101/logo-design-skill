# Practice Drills

Short exercises to assign between projects or as the "practise next" item in a wrap-up. Each has a goal, a
brief, a time box and what to bring back for review. Pick the drill that targets the weakness you've seen —
recurring mistakes in the design journal are the best guide.

| Drill | Trains | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1. 30 marks from one letter | Range, letterform invention | 60–90 min |
| 2. Negative-space drills | Figure/ground, economy | 45 min |
| 3. Memory redraw | Construction, proportion, observation | 30 min |
| 4. One-colour only | Form over colour | 45 min |
| 5. 16 px challenge | Reduction, small-size thinking | 45 min |
| 6. Type-pairing drill | Typographic judgement | 45 min |
| 7. One-letter change | Wordmark ownership | 45 min |
| 8. Reduction ladder | Editing, simplicity | 30 min |
| 9. Word map sprint | Idea generation beyond the obvious | 20 min |
| 10. Shelf test study | Distinctiveness, category awareness | 30 min |
| 11. Daily thumbnails | Volume, speed, loosening up | 15 min/day |
| 12. Rationale in 60 words | Presenting, clarity of idea | 20 min |

---

### 1. 30 marks from one letter
**Brief:** Choose one letter (start with a structurally rich one: a, g, k, r, s, w). Draw 30 different marks
from it, thumbnail size, in pen — no erasing. Force range: solid, line, stencil, negative space, ligature with a
second shape, rotated, built from one primitive, built on a grid, broken, stacked.
**Bring back:** a photo of the page. Review for range (how many genuinely different ideas vs. versions), and the
three with the most potential. *Reading:* Evamy's *Logotype*.

### 2. Negative-space drills
**Brief:** (a) Draw 10 pairs of letters whose counters or gaps form a third shape. (b) Take one simple object and
make it appear only in the gap between two other shapes. Keep it readable at thumbnail size.
**Bring back:** photos; say which read without explanation. *Principle:* `visual-techniques.md` §8.

### 3. Memory redraw
**Brief:** Pick a classic mark (e.g. from *Identify* or *Logo Modernism*). Look at it for 30 seconds, then put it
away and redraw it from memory. Then compare with the original: list every difference in proportion, angle,
weight and spacing. Study copy rules apply (`inspiration-sources.md` §4): label it, never publish or use it.
**Bring back:** your redraw, the list of differences, and the one construction lesson you learned.

### 4. One-colour only
**Brief:** Design a mark for a fictional brief using one flat colour — no tints, no gradients, no second colour —
and it must still express the brand's warmth or energy. Then test it reversed (white on black).
**Bring back:** the SVG; we'll run `preview_sheet.py` and check the reversed version for irradiation.
*Principle:* `color.md` §1, `testing-checklist.md` §2. *Reading:* Albers, *Interaction of Color*.

### 5. 16 px challenge
**Brief:** Take one of your own marks and make a version that works on a 16 × 16 pixel grid — draw it on graph
paper, one square per pixel. What must go? What must get bigger?
**Bring back:** the pixel drawing + your SVG small-size cut. We'll compare them at true size in the test sheet.
*Principle:* `principles.md` §2.8.

### 6. Type-pairing drill
**Brief:** Set the same brand name in 10 typefaces from different classifications (`typography.md` §2). For each,
write the brand personality it suggests in 3 words. Then pair your symbol with the best 3 and adjust spacing by
eye. Use Fonts In Use to see how the category sets its type.
**Bring back:** one sheet with all 10 + the 3 lockups. *Reading:* Lupton, *Thinking with Type* (anatomy, spacing).

### 7. One-letter change
**Brief:** Set a brand name in a plain typeface. Make 12 versions, each changing only **one** thing in **one**
letter (a cut, a join, a swapped form, a stretched terminal). The name must stay readable.
**Bring back:** the 12; we'll pick which change adds ownership without hurting legibility (the letter test in
`testing-checklist.md` §3).

### 8. Reduction ladder
**Brief:** Draw one pictorial subject in 6 steps from detailed to the simplest recognisable form. Stop when the
next step would make it unrecognisable. Which step is the logo?
**Bring back:** the ladder. *Principle:* `principles.md` §4 (simple, not plain). *Reading:* *Identify*.

### 9. Word map sprint
**Brief:** 10 minutes, one brand word, 60+ associations — force yourself past the first 20 obvious ones. Then 10
minutes to circle 5 intersections of unrelated branches and write each as a one-sentence concept.
**Bring back:** the map. We'll check how many concepts avoid the category's clichés.

### 10. Shelf test study
**Brief:** Collect 8 marks from one category (a street of cafés, your local bakeries, yoga studios in your city).
Put them in a grid in greyscale. Write what they share (shapes, type, colour habits) and where the open space is.
**Bring back:** the grid + your notes. This becomes the clichés list for your next brief in that category.

### 11. Daily thumbnails
**Brief:** 15 minutes a day for a week: 10 thumbnails for a random word (pull one from a dictionary). No judging
during; circle one at the end of each day.
**Bring back:** the week's 7 circled thumbnails. Look for how your range changes by day 7.

### 12. Rationale in 60 words
**Brief:** Write the rationale for one of your finished marks in 60 words: the problem, the idea, why it fits.
No adjectives about the design itself ("clean", "modern", "sleek") — only what it does.
**Bring back:** the paragraph; we'll critique it as the client and as a senior designer (Stage 6).
